:::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::: questions 

- How can you assess the characteristics of a dataset and determine the appropriate statistical analyses to undergo?
- How do you visualize different types of data to reveal important or interesting characteristics?
- How can we streamline or standardize analyses to save time and reduce error when conducting the same analysis across datasets or over time?

::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::

::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::::: objectives

- Mapping and assessing data distribution is essential to selecting the appropriate statistical approach--histograms are helpful tools to visualize data distributions. 
- Continuous and discrete variables must be represented differently: continuous data rely on accurate scaling and styles that connect observations, whereas discrete data are best visualized with individual points or bars to illustrate the gaps between data points.
- For- and while-loops are key to accomplishing iteration while minimizing error. Functions allow us to combine iteration of tasks in a standardized way that is repeatable and shareable.

## Introduction

### Challenge 1: Multiple-choice Clicker questions & Discussion
  * **Demonstration**:
      * Session will proceed from a review of basic plot types (scatter, histogram, line, etc.), including code to generate them, to activate prior plotting knowledge (assumed to be basic)
      * Once review of plot types is complete, content will be shared on basic statistical theory
          * What a distribution is/represents
          * Types of distributions
          * Assumptions of normality
          * Define parametric versus non-parametric
          * Analyses appropriate to different distributions and data types
      * Instructor will then demonstrate these skills by doing the following:
          * Writing some code to render a simple histogram with working dataset's distribution
          * Display the histogram/distribution and talk through the assessment of distribution type: is the data normally distributed? bimodal? skewed? 
          * Once distribution is determined, instructor will share whether parametric or non-parametric tests are appropriate and provide explanation as to why, outlining the assumptions that could be made about the data
          * To illustrate the consequences of selecting the wrong test, instructor will run a parametric test and a non-parametric test on the same data (a simple subset) and walk through a test results comparison with the class
      * Exercise with multiple-choice clicker & discussion will commence
      * Learners should have a basic grasp of distribution assessment & test-matching at this point, which will be necessary to complete the exercise
